# Writing style

Goal: text a hiring manager reads as written by a capable person, not generated.

## Ground rules
- Facts only. Every claim, tool and number comes from my CV. Motivation comes from the posting and my CV: never invent a contact, a visit, an event or an article I read.
- Specific beats general: "cut processing time from 6 hours to 40 minutes", not "significantly improved performance".
- Plain verbs: built, wrote, designed, measured, fixed, ran, tested, reduced, cared for, taught.
- Mix sentence lengths. Avoid stacking three adjectives or three-item lists in every sentence.
- No em-dashes. Use a full stop, comma or brackets.

## Cover letter
One page, plain paragraphs, no bullet lists. A full page is 450-500 words in 5 paragraphs; when the letter length is set to short (300-350 words in 4), the gap becomes one sentence in paragraph 3.
1. Hook: the role and one concrete reason for this employer, taken from the posting (their product, their problem, their team's task). Never "I am writing to apply".
2. Evidence: the posting's top requirement. What I did, the number or outcome, and one sentence on why it matters for their task.
3. Evidence: the next requirement, the same way, from another job or project. Include one detail only I could write: a concrete moment from my CV.
4. Honest gap: one thing the posting asks that my CV doesn't show, said plainly, with the closest real experience and how I would close it. When nothing important is missing, a third piece of evidence instead.
5. Close: which of their tasks I can take on first, my earliest start and, where relevant, my work-permit status (for student jobs also my hours or availability). A short thanks.
- Greeting to a named person when the posting names one, otherwise a neutral team greeting in the letter's language. The sign-off and my name are added by the letter design: never write them in the last paragraph.
