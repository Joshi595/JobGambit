# CV instructions

## Ground rules
- Facts only: every claim, tool and number comes from my CV. Never invent metrics, tools, team sizes or outcomes.
- Never claim a skill, tool or language level my CV doesn't show, even when the posting asks for it, and not reworded either. A gap belongs in the letter's honest-gap paragraph, never in the CV.
- Specific beats general: a number or a concrete result over "significantly improved".
- Plain verbs: built, wrote, designed, measured, fixed, ran, tested, reduced.
- Interview test: I must be able to explain every line in an interview without "what I actually meant was…".

## Summary
- Two or three sentences for this job. The first names the kind of role and my strongest proof for it (a result with a number where my CV has one).
- No adjectives about myself, and never a job title I never held.

## CV lines
- Start each line with a past-tense verb (present tense only for my current role).
- One idea per line, one or two rows, the result or purpose at the end.
- Use the posting's exact terms where my facts truly support them (applicant-tracking systems match literal words).
- Never claim a bigger role than my CV says ("Led" where I "Built"), and never change job titles or dates.

## What to show
- Lead with what the posting asks first.
- Lines per role: 3-5 for my current or most relevant role, 1-2 for older or less relevant ones; a role unrelated to this job keeps its title, dates and at most one line.
- Projects: the 3 that best prove the posting's must-haves. Before leaving one out, check whether it carries a term the posting names.
- Skills: the categories the posting cares about first, its tools first inside each. Leave out skills that don't help for this job, so the list reads in a few seconds; never add one.
- Page budget: fill the page limit, never more. When space runs short, cut in this order: skills nobody asked for, the weakest lines of older roles, a sentence of the summary, then lines of the third project.

## Per job type
- Working student, internship or thesis: say I am enrolled (degree, university, expected graduation where my CV gives it) and keep coursework and student projects that match the posting.
- PhD or research job: lead with the thesis and the methods (experiments, evaluation, simulation, publications); keep research interests that match the group's topic.
- Full-time job: lead with outcomes from real work (in production, with customers, in the field); coursework only where it is the strongest proof.
